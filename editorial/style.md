# Guide Style Profile

This profile defines the guide's preferred voice. Follow
[`writing-rules.md`](writing-rules.md) for evidence, scope, and review rules.
Technical accuracy takes priority over stylistic consistency.

## Voice and Structure

- Write for research programmers with varied software-engineering experience.
- Lead with the practical problem, then give the recommendation, its reason,
  and important limitations.
- Use direct, concrete prose. Define specialized terms and abbreviations on
  first use.
- Keep paragraphs focused. Use lists for procedures, constraints, comparisons,
  and checklists.
- Use cautious, conditional language for tradeoffs. State the comparison axis,
  such as reproducibility, portability, installation speed, or maintenance.

## Examples and Terminology

- Prefer small, realistic examples that readers can adapt. Explain inputs,
  outputs, placeholders, and platform assumptions when they affect the result.
- Keep terminology and capitalization consistent within and across chapters.
- Distinguish universal practices from optional choices and
  environment-specific guidance.
- Avoid inflated claims, vague importance statements, unexplained jargon, and
  generic transitions that do not clarify the next step.

## Meaning-Sensitive Edits

- Preserve commands, code, paths, versions, numbers, units, links, headings,
  and stated uncertainty unless the task explicitly changes them.
- Do not strengthen a recommendation or conclusion while editing for style.
- Flag a sentence that needs a technical or editorial decision instead of
  silently resolving it.
